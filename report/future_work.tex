In terms of future work, there are several avenues that could be explored in relation to this research. As discussed in \autoref{Discussion}, the model we have created is fairly limited, but it serves as a useful starting point for future exploration, which we believe could take one of two directions: expanding the model, or beginning testing on real systems.

\subsection{Expanding the Model}
\label{ExpandingTheModel}



\subsection{Real System Testing}
\label{RealSystemTesting}

Possibly the most interesting and by far the most difficult paths forward would be to begin testing/simulating on a real system. By real system, we of course mean a Linux adjacent Virtual Machine, which would allow us to have a more tangible and realistic environment to test our theories on. This of course offers the greatest potential for insight, but as we also look towards the future for technological advancements, it is also the most difficult to achieve, as it would require a significant amount of work and time to just set up, let alone to run and train a model on it. In addition to this, it is also a demanding endeavor in terms of computing resources, as simulating a real system with two agents running around and thinking of their next move is a computationally intensive task. Not to mention, the fact that we would need to run this simulation multiple times to actually have the model improve and learn from it, which would further increase the computational demands and time requirements of this path forward.

There is of course a potential legal and ethical aspect to this as well, as it would be simulating a real attack on a real system, which could potentially be used for malicious purposes, or in the unlikely scenario that there would be a way for the simulated agents to leave the virtual machine, they would also have been trained on how to do real damage to a computer system. This is of course a risk that would need to be mitigated, but it is still a concern that should be taken into account when considering this path forward.

This in all is of course overshadowed by the very real potential that this simulation is not possible to develop in the first place, but the potential insights that could be gained from it are also very significant, as it would allow us to test our theories in a more realistic environment, and potentially discover new strategies and interactions that we had not previously considered. It would also allow us to see how the model holds up in a real-world scenario, and whether the conclusions we have drawn from it are still valid when applied to a real system.
